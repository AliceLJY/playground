溯源

这一页讲：画是照什么画的，哪些有依据，哪些是我们加的。

诗

空山不见人，但闻人语响。
返景入深林，复照青苔上。

王维《鹿柴》，《辋川集》二十首之一。王维是盛唐人。「返景」是夕阳返照的光，所以画里的时间定在傍晚，日头在右边，很低，越往山里走越低。

为什么是四幅画

古人看山水叫「卧游」。手卷是一段一段展开看的，人不动，景在换，叫移步换景。这里一句诗配一幅画，一幅一幅往山里走：远望空山；走到林边，听见人语、看不见人；进了深林，斜阳照进来；低头，返照落在青苔上。

王维的画

王维没有公认的真迹传世。画史上说他有两路：一路学李思训的着色，就是青绿山水；一路承吴道子，带墨韵。宋代苏轼说他「诗中有画，画中有诗」；明代董其昌以禅论画，分南北宗，北宗推李思训，南宗推王维。传在他名下的几件作品真伪另当别论，但可以从中窥见画风。这一卷取的是勾线设色这一路；设色照下面说的唐摹本，很淡，大半是绢色，没有铺石青石绿。

画法的依据

诗是什么年代，画就用什么年代的画法。这一卷依的看法是：唐代山水以线为主、以色为辅，先用匀细的线勾出山石树木的轮廓，再平涂颜色；轮廓线以内不皴；山头的苔和树丛，先用细线一簇簇圈出来再填色。后来山水里那种由浓到淡的墨色渲染，是五代末、北宋初才成熟的，所以这里不用。画史上对唐代设色有没有晕染另有说法，这里取的是从严的一种。

所以这四幅画里没有皴，没有渲染，没有明暗，颜色很少、很淡、很平，大半是绢的本色。主要照的是《女史箴图》唐摹本（大英博物馆藏）里画山的那一段：原作传为东晋顾恺之，现存这一卷一般认为是唐人摹的，看的是唐人怎么下笔。第三幅画的时候另拿敦煌莫高窟第 217 窟盛唐壁画里的山石作旁证。都只学画法，不抄构图。

这一卷的第一版用过带渲染的青绿，请懂画的人看过，指出年代不对，四幅都重画了。

字

卷尾的诗用楷书，照的是唐代欧阳询《九成宫醴泉铭》的笔法（参考宋拓本，台北故宫博物院藏）。把诗题在画面上、当成画的一部分，是宋元以后才成形的做法；唐画上怎么题，我们没有查实，所以诗不写在画上，放在卷尾的素纸上。

哪些是今人加的

这四幅画照唐人勾线平涂的画法，画上不画光，也不画影。屏幕上那片返照、苔上的树影、风里动的枝叶、远处的云气、人语和脚步声，还有镜头往画里走，都是我们加在画外面的一层，画本身是平的、静的。

怎么做的

画和字都是 AI 照上面的画法新作的，不是古迹，也不是哪一幅古画的复制。画没有拆开重画，只给每幅配了一张深度图，让近处比远处多动一点。

怎么看：按住屏幕（或按住空格、方向键）是往前走，松开它自己慢慢展；左右拨、滚滚轮可以来回看。右下角是声音开关。走到头有「再看一遍」。

Sources

What these paintings follow, what has a basis, and what we added.

The poem

Empty mountain. No one in sight.
Only the sound of voices, somewhere.
Evening light returns into the deep wood,
and shines again on the green moss.

"Deer Park" (Lù Zhài) by Wang Wei, who lived in eighth-century China under the Tang dynasty. It is one of twenty short poems about places on his country estate at Wangchuan; Deer Park is the name of one of those places, and there are no deer in the poem. The "returning light" is the low light of sunset, so the paintings are set in the evening, with the sun low on the right and lower in every scene. The English here is our own plain rendering.

Why four paintings

Old viewers called looking at a landscape painting woyou, travelling while lying down: the body stays where it is and the mind walks into the picture. A handscroll is unrolled a stretch at a time, so the viewer stays put while the scenery changes. Here each line of the poem has one painting, and you walk further into the hills with each: the empty mountain from afar; the edge of the wood, where voices carry but nobody is seen; the deep wood, with late sun slanting in; then the ground at your feet, light on moss.

Wang Wei as a painter

No painting accepted as from Wang Wei's own hand survives. Chinese accounts of painting describe two sides to his work: one coloured, in the manner of Li Sixun; one closer to Wu Daozi, with the life of the ink line. In the Song dynasty Su Shi wrote that there is painting in his poems and poetry in his paintings; in the Ming, Dong Qichang named him the founder of the "Southern school". This scroll follows the outlined-and-coloured side, with very light colour.

What the manner is based on

A poem should be painted in the manner of its own period. The view followed here is that Tang landscape is line first and colour second: rocks and trees are drawn in fine, even outline and then filled with flat colour; nothing is textured inside the outline; moss and foliage on the peaks are small clusters outlined one by one and then filled. The graded ink washes of later landscape matured only around the tenth century, so they are not used. Historians differ on how much graded colour Tang painters used; this takes the stricter view.

So these four paintings have no texture strokes, no graded washes and no light and shade; the colour is sparse, pale and flat, and most of the surface is bare silk. The main model is the mountain passage of the Admonitions of the Court Instructress scroll in the British Museum: the original is attributed to Gu Kaizhi of the fourth century, and the surviving scroll is generally thought to be a Tang copy, so it shows how a Tang hand drew. The third painting also looked to the rocks in the High Tang murals of Mogao Cave 217 at Dunhuang. Only the manner was followed, never the composition.

The first version of this scroll used washed blue-and-green. Someone who knows painting saw it and said the period was wrong, and all four were repainted.

The writing

The poem at the end is in regular script after Ouyang Xun, the early Tang calligrapher, following his Jiucheng Palace Sweet Spring Inscription (from a Song-dynasty rubbing in the National Palace Museum, Taipei). Writing a poem onto the picture itself became usual only from the Song and Yuan onward, and we could not confirm how it was done in the Tang, so the poem is not on the paintings. It waits on plain paper at the tail of the scroll.

What is modern

These paintings follow the Tang way of outline and flat colour, and no light or shadow is painted on them. The patch of returning light on screen, the tree shadows on the moss, the leaves moving in the wind, the far mist, the voices and footsteps, and the view walking into the picture are all a layer we added outside the paintings. The paintings themselves are flat and still.

How it was made

The paintings and the writing were newly made with AI in the manner described above. They are not old works and not copies of any one painting. The paintings were not cut apart or repainted; each has a depth map so that near things move slightly more than far things.

How to look: press and hold (or hold Space or an arrow key) to walk forward; let go and it unrolls slowly by itself; swipe sideways or scroll to look back and forth. The sound switch is at the bottom right. At the end you can go round again.
